How much of the tree of life is on your plate?

Paste a recipe or name a dish. Every ingredient gets mapped to the organism it came from, the eukaryote tree gets pruned to what's on the plate, and the branch lengths get added up: millions of years of evolution per meal.

Data

Tree version demo-0.1. The built-in tree has 0 edible taxa and 0 Myr of branch length. Node ages in this build are approximate placeholders: deep splits follow published ranges loosely, shallow ones are educated guesses. Do not quote them. To test another tree, load a Newick whose tip labels are ids plus a CSV with columns id, common_name, species, lineage, clade, aliases; clade is one of animal, fungus, plant, green, red, brown, other; branch lengths in Myr, ultrametric.

Method

PD is Faith's (1992) phylogenetic diversity: the sum of branch lengths of the minimum subtree connecting the meal's taxa to the root of the tree. Richness is the count of distinct taxa. Coverage divides the meal's PD by the total branch length of the tracked edible tree, so it depends on how many edible taxa are tracked. The span below the common ancestor is the same sum without the path from the meal's MRCA to the root; it is zero for a single-organism meal. Prokaryotes are excluded on purpose. Amounts are ignored; an abundance-weighted variant (Chao, Chiu and Jost's phylogenetic Hill numbers) is the natural next step.

Ingredient parsing uses Claude through the viewer's own account; the page then resolves each organism against its own taxon table, so nothing outside the table can be scored. Tree data: placeholder ages, not citable. Tree version demo-0.1; scores change when the tree does. Built as a prototype for a phylogenetic-diversity diet score.
